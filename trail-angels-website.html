About the Project
A month in the heart of Tuscany — for nature, for people, for Europe.

Trail Angels is a one-month ESC volunteering project taking place in the rural areas of Tuscany, Italy. International teams of young people live and work together to maintain and improve hiking trails used by locals, pilgrims, and tourists every year.

The project combines environmental conservation, cultural exchange, and community engagement. Volunteers work side by side to restore paths, support pilgrims on ancient routes, and bring life to local communities — all while developing personal skills and gaining a unique international experience.

The work is physical and outdoors, but deeply rewarding. Volunteers literally make nature more accessible for everyone, living surrounded by mountains, forests, and the iconic Tuscan landscape.

The project is organised by CNV Lucca (Centro Nazionale Per Il Volontariato), one of Italy's leading volunteering organisations with over 30 years of experience in civic engagement, ESC and Erasmus+ youth mobility programmes.

Volunteering Periods & Locations
Three distinct periods across some of Tuscany's most beautiful territories. Exact dates may vary slightly — confirmed upon selection.
Period 1
01 May – 30 May 2026
📍 Castelnuovo di Garfagnana

Nestled in the Garfagnana mountains of northern Tuscany, this medieval town sits between the Apuan Alps and the Apennines. Volunteers work on mountain trails surrounded by chestnut forests and crystal-clear rivers in one of Italy's most spectacular yet undiscovered natural areas.

Period 2
01 June – 30 June 2026
📍 La Brilla – Porta del Parco

Located in the Massarosa area, La Brilla serves as a gateway to the Migliarino-San Rossore-Massaciuccoli Regional Park. Volunteers work at the interface between nature reserves, lake ecosystems, and Mediterranean coastal forests.

Period 3
01 July – 30 July 2026
📍 Fucecchio

Fucecchio is a historic town close to Florence and Pisa. Accommodation for this period is provided at Ostello Ponte dè Medici and its dependance Villa Ruben, both located in Fucecchio (FI).

Some projects begin with an idea and slowly become something much bigger than anyone originally imagined. Trail Angels is one of those projects.

Between 2023 and 2026, young people from different countries came together in Tuscany through the European Solidarity Corps with a shared purpose: to contribute their time and energy to protecting the environment, caring for historic walking routes and supporting the communities that welcomed them.

Over the course of the project, different international teams became part of the Trail Angels story. Each group arrived with its own energy, personalities, languages and cultures, but the idea connecting them remained the same. Volunteering was not only about travelling somewhere new. It was about becoming part of a place, contributing to it and leaving something positive behind.

Much of the Trail Angels journey took place outdoors, along the historic paths and natural areas of Tuscany. Volunteers worked together to clear vegetation, remove branches and debris, collect litter and help keep walking routes accessible and welcoming.

The work was often simple and practical, but its impact could be seen immediately. A path that needed attention could look completely different after a group of volunteers had spent time working together. Bags of rubbish disappeared from natural areas, overgrown sections became easier to walk, and the landscapes surrounding the trails received the care they needed.

Behind these activities was an important idea: the places we enjoy are also places we have a responsibility to protect.

For the Trail Angels, this responsibility became closely connected with the Via Francigena, one of Europe's most historic pilgrimage routes. For centuries, people have travelled along these paths, and today walkers and pilgrims from around the world continue to follow them through Tuscany.

Working along these routes gave the volunteers the opportunity to meet some of the people travelling them. These encounters often provided a simple reminder of why maintaining the paths matters. Every clean section of trail and every small improvement can become part of someone else's journey.

In 2024, this curiosity about the people walking the route developed into Pilgrim Talks, a three-part interview series created together with the European Association of the Via Francigena. Instead of simply watching pilgrims pass by, the volunteers stopped to listen to their stories.

They discovered people walking for many different reasons. Some were searching for adventure, others were taking on personal challenges, and some were simply looking for a different way to experience the world. Every person brought a different story to the same road.

The volunteers also collected practical information that could be useful to future walkers, including information about water points and places to rest. By bringing this information together in a map, the team was able to transform its own experience on the trail into something that could support other people travelling the route.

Environmental action remained one of the strongest elements of Trail Angels throughout the project. Clean-up activities repeatedly showed how much waste can accumulate along walking routes and in natural areas. Volunteers collected bag after bag of litter, sometimes surprised by how much could be found in places that appeared untouched at first sight.

These activities also became an opportunity to communicate a wider message about environmental responsibility. Cleaning a path is important, but preventing rubbish from reaching it in the first place is even better. Through their work and communication activities, the Trail Angels encouraged others to respect the environment and remember that protecting shared natural spaces is everyone's responsibility.

As the project developed, the volunteers also discovered different sides of Tuscany. Beyond the places most commonly associated with the region, they experienced forests, countryside, historic paths and quieter natural areas. Walking and volunteering provided a completely different perspective from conventional tourism. Instead of simply visiting a destination, the volunteers spent time getting to know the landscapes and communities around them.

This slower way of experiencing Tuscany became an important part of the Trail Angels story. It showed that travelling can also mean taking the time to walk, observe and connect with a place rather than simply moving from one attraction to another.

Some volunteers shared these experiences through online content and podcast episodes, giving others an opportunity to hear directly what international volunteering was like. Photos, videos and stories also helped bring the experience beyond Tuscany and introduce more young people to environmental volunteering and the opportunities available through the European Solidarity Corps.

However, not everything that made Trail Angels meaningful happened on the trails. An equally important part of the project happened in kitchens, shared houses and around dinner tables.

Every new group brought together young people who often came from completely different backgrounds. They arrived speaking different languages and carrying different habits, expectations and ideas about everyday life. Suddenly, they had to learn how to live together.

There were groceries to buy, meals to prepare and responsibilities to share. There were cultural differences to navigate and misunderstandings to solve. Sometimes there was chaos, but there was also plenty of laughter.

Cultural evenings became opportunities for volunteers to introduce their countries through food, traditions and stories. At other times, cultural exchange happened naturally during an ordinary dinner or conversation. These everyday moments became just as important as the organised activities.

People who arrived as strangers learned how to cooperate. They discovered things about countries they may never have visited and cultures they may previously have known very little about. Friendships developed between people whose paths might never have crossed without the European Solidarity Corps.

In this way, Trail Angels became a project about the environment, but also about people. It was about learning that solidarity can take many forms. Sometimes it means spending hours outdoors cleaning a trail. Sometimes it means supporting a local activity or helping at a community event. Sometimes it simply means noticing where help is needed and being willing to contribute.

As the volunteers became more connected with the communities around them, their role could also extend beyond the original environmental activities. Taking part in local volunteering initiatives and supporting community events created opportunities for international volunteers to meet people who had been contributing to their communities for years.

These moments showed that international volunteering does not need to exist separately from local life. When volunteers are given the opportunity to participate, listen and contribute, they can become part of a community rather than simply visitors passing through it.

Looking at the Trail Angels journey from 2023 to 2026, its story is ultimately made up of many small actions. It is found in every bag of rubbish removed from nature, every path cleared and every walker who finds the route a little easier to follow. It is found in conversations with pilgrims and in the stories they shared. It is found in the volunteers who arrived in Tuscany without knowing one another and left with friendships that crossed borders.

It is found in shared meals, cultural evenings, difficult working days, spontaneous adventures and all the ordinary moments that never appear in an official project description but often become the memories people keep for the longest.

The historic paths running through Tuscany existed long before the first Trail Angels arrived, and people will continue to walk them long into the future. The contribution of one volunteering project may represent only a small chapter in their much longer history. But small contributions matter.

Between 2023 and 2026, young people from different parts of Europe and beyond came to Tuscany and chose to give their time to something they believed was worthwhile. Together, they helped care for the environment, supported walking routes, contributed to local communities and shared their experiences with others. At the same time, they learned from one another and discovered what it means to build a community across languages, cultures and borders.

Perhaps this is the most important result of Trail Angels. The project shows that solidarity does not always need to begin with something extraordinary. It can begin with a group of people putting on their gloves and deciding to clean a path together. From there, it can grow into conversations, friendships, cultural exchange and a feeling of connection with a place that was once completely unfamiliar.

The trails remain, waiting for the next people to walk them. And wherever the Trail Angels go next, a small part of their story will remain in Tuscany — along the paths they walked, in the places they helped to care for and in the communities they became part of along the way.

To every volunteer, partner, community member, pilgrim and person who contributed to this journey: thank you for being part of the Trail Angels story.

In 2023, a new adventure began in Tuscany as the first team of Trail Angels came together through the European Solidarity Corps. Young people from different countries and backgrounds arrived with the same goal: to spend their time doing something meaningful for the environment while discovering a new place and experiencing international community life.

The main focus of the project was caring for the historic walking routes that cross Tuscany and the natural environment surrounding them. The volunteers took part in practical outdoor activities, clearing vegetation and branches from the paths, collecting litter and helping to keep the trails clean and accessible for the people who use them.

The work could sometimes be physically demanding, especially under the Tuscan sun, but it was also rewarding to see the results. After working together on a section of a path, the difference was often immediately visible. Knowing that the same path would later be used by walkers and pilgrims gave the activities an additional sense of purpose.

The project also offered an opportunity to learn more about the Via Francigena and the long history behind the routes running through Tuscany. Pilgrims have been following these roads for centuries, and people from all over the world continue to walk them today. By taking care of the trails and their surroundings, the volunteers became part of this ongoing story in their own small way.

Of course, being a Trail Angel was not only about the hours spent outdoors. Living together as an international group quickly became an equally important part of the experience. The volunteers cooked and shopped together, shared responsibilities and spent their free time discovering Tuscany. Evenings often turned into informal cultural exchanges, with everyone sharing stories about their countries, teaching each other words in different languages and learning about traditions they had never encountered before.

As time passed, the group of strangers who had arrived in Tuscany gradually became a real team. New friendships were formed, countless memories were created and the first chapter of the Trail Angels story was written.

The volunteers may eventually return home, but the paths they helped to care for will continue to welcome new walkers and pilgrims. And for the Trail Angels project, this is only the beginning.

Over time, cultural differences become something to celebrate rather than something separating the group. Everyone brings something different to the experience, and it is precisely this diversity that makes international volunteering so special.

The new team continues the work started by the Trail Angels before them, leaving its own contribution to the paths and communities of Tuscany while creating a completely new set of memories along the way.

The Via Francigena is made up of countless individual stories, and for a short time, the story of the Trail Angels became part of it too.

One of the most visible challenges along popular walking routes is also one of the most preventable: litter. Plastic bottles, food packaging, cans and other waste can quickly accumulate along paths and in natural areas, affecting both the landscape and the people who come to enjoy it.

In 2024, this became an important focus for the Trail Angels in Tuscany. Equipped with gloves and rubbish bags, the volunteers regularly headed out along the trails to collect waste and help restore the surrounding environment.

The results of these activities were often immediately visible. Bag after bag of rubbish was removed from places where it did not belong, showing both the scale of the problem and how much can be achieved when a group of people decides to take action.

However, the message of the clean-up activities went beyond simply collecting what others had left behind. The volunteers also wanted to encourage walkers, visitors and local communities to think about their own responsibility towards the environment. Historic trails such as the Via Francigena are shared spaces, and protecting them depends on everyone who uses them.

"Help us keep it clean" became a simple message behind many of the team's activities. It was a reminder that caring for nature does not always require complicated solutions. Sometimes, it begins with respecting the places we visit and making sure we leave nothing behind.

Outside the volunteering activities, the international group continued to experience everyday life together in Tuscany. Shared meals, group shopping and cultural evenings became an important part of the project. Volunteers introduced their countries through food, stories and traditions, turning ordinary evenings into opportunities to discover different parts of Europe without leaving the dinner table.

Living together also brought its own lessons in cooperation and solidarity. Sharing responsibilities, adapting to different habits and finding solutions together became part of the experience just as much as the environmental work.

By combining practical action with a simple message about individual responsibility, the Trail Angels hope to leave the paths of Tuscany cleaner and encourage others to think twice before leaving waste behind. After all, keeping these beautiful places clean is something we can all contribute to.

When people think about Tuscany, they often imagine famous cities, Renaissance art, vineyards and rolling hills. However, beyond these familiar images, there is another side of the region that many visitors never have the opportunity to experience.

In 2025, the Trail Angels are discovering this quieter and greener Tuscany while volunteering along historic walking and pilgrimage routes surrounded by forests, mountains and small communities.

Working in this environment brings its own challenges. The paths can be steep and vegetation grows quickly, which means regular maintenance is essential to keep the routes accessible. The volunteers spend their working time clearing overgrown sections, removing litter and walking along the trails to monitor their condition.

The work requires energy, and some days are more physically demanding than others. At the same time, it offers the team an opportunity to experience the landscape in a way that would be difficult as ordinary tourists. Walking through forests and following historic paths creates a much closer connection with the places the volunteers are helping to protect.

The experience also highlights a different way of travelling. While many of Europe's most famous destinations are becoming increasingly crowded, rural areas and lesser-known walking routes offer visitors an opportunity to slow down and experience a destination differently.

Instead of rushing from one attraction to another, travellers can walk, observe the landscape and discover communities that are often overlooked by mass tourism. For the Trail Angels, this slower approach has become a natural part of everyday life in Tuscany.

The volunteering experience continues outside the trails as well. The team spends time hiking, cooking, exploring and sharing everyday life as an international group. Volunteers also have opportunities to talk about their experiences and reflect on what it means to live and volunteer in another country.

Some of these conversations have even found their way into podcast episodes, allowing volunteers to share their experiences in their own words and give others a more personal understanding of international volunteering.

Through their work, the Trail Angels are not only helping to care for the paths they walk but also discovering a side of Tuscany that is easy to miss. Sometimes, stepping away from the most familiar destinations is exactly what allows us to understand a place better.

Trail Angels (Project No. 101093414) is funded by the European Union through the European Solidarity Corps (ESC) Programme, managed by the European Education and Culture Executive Agency (EACEA) on behalf of the European Commission.

The European Solidarity Corps is the European Union's initiative for young people who want to volunteer or work in projects that benefit communities and people throughout Europe. Through ESC, the EU invests in young people's personal development, intercultural competences, and active citizenship.

This project contributes to the ESC priorities of environmental sustainability, social inclusion, and intercultural dialogue — bringing together young people from different European countries to make a tangible contribution to the conservation of Italy's natural and cultural heritage.

Centro Nazionale per il Volontariato
Lucca · Italy

CNV Lucca is one of Italy's leading volunteering organisations, founded in 1985 and based in Lucca, Tuscany. We promote voluntary action, active citizenship, and youth mobility through ESC, Erasmus+, and international cooperation programmes.
